# Tidy Up

Tidy Up keeps the libraries on the media drive (`MEDIA_ROOT`) sorted, tagged, and lean: music, books, films, TV, and audiobooks. It has its own screen in the app, and `homelab media` does the same from a terminal. Everything it throws away goes to the Trash (the Recycle Bin on Windows), never straight away. On Windows, a USB drive, SD card or network share has no Recycle Bin, so what Tidy Up throws away there goes to a hidden `.tidy-trash` folder at the top of that drive, a folder a day; nothing empties it, so empty it yourself to free the space, and before the drive goes to someone else.

## The screen

The first time (no library anywhere, and no drive Tidy Up is waiting for), **Inbox** opens on **Where should your libraries live?**: this computer's own disk (a `Media` folder in your home folder) and every drive plugged in, each with its space and the libraries already on it; the drive Duplicati backs up to can't be picked; **Another folder…** for a NAS or one folder for everything. The one picked lists where each library goes on it and what's there (found 1,204 artists, a Calibre library, new). **Use it** keeps `MEDIA_ROOT` and runs `homelab fix`, which lists the apps that read the libraries (Jellyfin, Navidrome, Calibre-Web…) and recreates them once, after asking; nothing is moved or copied. A library kept elsewhere with `TIDY_<KIND>_LIBRARY` stays there. After that, **Change where your libraries live…** under Tidy up now opens it again, as does **Choose another…** beside a media drive that isn't plugged in. Otherwise **Inbox** opens on **Drop anything here**: the folders Tidy Up watches (Downloads and `DOWNLOADS_ROOT` until others are picked; **Add…** opens the folder picker, **Remove** is there while another is left, and one on a drive that's unplugged says *Not there now*), when it files what lands in them (*As things arrive*, every minute the folders change; *Once a day, 3 AM*; or *Only when I press it*, kept as `TIDY_INBOX_FOLDERS` and `TIDY_INBOX_WHEN`), and **Tidy up now** across the card, which files what's waiting at once. Under it, when there is one, **Needs a look**: each download no engine could place (a video whose name says nothing, another cut of a film you have, a library copy that can't be read) and each one Undo put back, with why, from the inbox's list in `data/media-needs.json` (`HOMELAB_MEDIA_NEEDS`). A video has **It's a film…** (its title, and year if known) and **It's an episode…** (the show and season, and the episode for a single file; in either, **Look it up** asks Radarr for a film or Sonarr for a show (with the key in its `config.xml`, while it runs), else Jellyfin's search (with `JELLYFIN_API_KEY`), and lists what it finds with its year, so a person picks *The Big Lebowski (1998)* rather than typing the year; picking is a help, not a must, and with nothing here to ask the sheet says so and files what's typed; a folder of episodes keeps each one's own number): Tidy Up renames it to that and files it (`homelab media inbox name <path> movie "<title>" [<year>]`, or `episode "<show>" <season> [<episode>]`), and the line in What I did today says *named by you, then filed*; one it still can't place stays, saying so. Songs that don't say which artist or album they are wait here too, rather than being filed into a folder with no name; with AcoustID set up (`fpcalc` and beets' `chroma`), beets first tries them by their sound, files the ones it matches, and leaves the rest where they are (`quiet_fallback: skip`, on top of the inbox's beets settings), so only those wait here, and a download that may stay isn't shrunk first. **Name these songs…** asks the album (and the artist, if known), **Look it up** asks MusicBrainz for the albums by that name (each with its artist, year, number of songs, format and country, so editions can be told apart), and **File them** writes the picked album's names onto the songs (title, artist, album, year, track and disc, and its MusicBrainz ids) and files them as that album (`homelab media inbox name <path> album <MusicBrainz release id>`). The songs are matched to the album by the track numbers in their names (*01 …*, *1-03 …*, a *CD2* folder), or in order when the counts agree; a different count with no numbers to go by, or a song far longer or shorter than its place on the album (another edition, most likely), stops it, and it stays, saying why. Only the two names typed are sent to MusicBrainz. A download that is or holds only a `.rar` or `.7z` (or their parts, `.r00`, `.7z.001`), which Tidy Up doesn't open, is listed once with *It's packed in a .rar file…*: open it (double-click it), and what's inside is filed on the next sweep; a `.zip` is unpacked by the sweep itself, and a `.rar` beside a download's film (its subtitles, say) is no reason to hold the film. Every one has **Show in Finder** (Explorer on Windows) and **Leave it** (`homelab media inbox leave <path>`), after which the inbox never files it. Only a download Needs a look lists can be named or left from the app. Then **What I did today**: a line for each download the inbox filed that day, newest first, saying what it is, where it went, and what was done (tags cleaned, lyrics added, too big so Tdarr shrinks it first, the better copy kept), from the inbox's record in `data/media-filed.jsonl` (`HOMELAB_MEDIA_FILED_LOG`). **Undo** (`homelab media inbox undo <id>`) moves just the files that download became back where it came from, a folder as a folder, and the inbox leaves them there until they change; a book added to Calibre has no Undo here. Below that, the rest answers three things in order. First, **Needs your OK**, beside the drive (a ring of what each library takes, and what is free): the next thing to do leads it, larger, under *Next up*, after only a drive to plug in or a tool to install. Then **Your libraries**, a tile for each in its own colour: what it holds, how far along it is where the record can say (songs with lyrics, films and episodes already lean), and what it waits on; a tile opens the library's tab, which starts with that library (its folder and figures, and for music its clean-up step by step: scanned, built, swapped in, checked, freed). Then the genres and **Recently done**. **Needs your OK** is the next thing each library waits on (a scan, a swap, films to shrink, books to look over), what it wins back (films and shows to shrink, extra copies, audiobooks to merge, from the last scan), and its one button. The first thing to do has the screen's one main button; a step that throws things away (freeing the old music library, removing extra copies) never does, however early it comes. Beside it, a ring of genres, with a dropdown for whose: your music's in your 14 genres (`homelab media music genre-counts`), your films' and shows' from Jellyfin or the `.nfo` files beside them (`movies genre-counts`, `tv genre-counts`), and your books' from each book's first tag in Calibre (`books genre-counts`, which only reads Calibre's database); the media drive with what each library takes and what is free (measured in the background, at most every six hours, into `~/.homelab-media/sizes.json`), and **Recently done**, with **Undo** on the music swap until the space is freed, on the books' last apply, and on whatever a run put in the Trash: each thing Tidy Up throws away is noted under its run in `data/media-trash.jsonl`, and Undo (`homelab media restore <run>`) puts back what is still in the Trash, never over something now in its place. Two runs don't offer it: Tdarr's collect (the smaller copy has the original's name) and freeing the old music library. On Windows, what went to the Recycle Bin is named instead, to restore from there; a USB or network drive's `.tidy-trash` is put back like a Mac's or Linux's Trash. Each library has its own tab: what could be tidier, its space, and its history. A music check that finds songs missing says how many and whose, with **Put the old library back…** and **Check again**; the space can't be freed until every song is found. Sizes are counted as the Finder and Explorer count a drive (1000 to a KB), the way the engines write them, so *412 GB won back* at the top and the *412 GB* a run freed, on the right of its line in Recently done (where Finances puts an amount; lines from before this say it in their words), are one figure; the first line counts the same things as **Needs your OK**, a tool to install included.

## The engines

The work is done by Python scripts in `tools/media`, run by the homelab with Python 3.8 or newer (3.10 to 3.14 for music run on this computer: its one-time setup installs beets and its helpers at the versions pinned in `tools/media/requirements`, each checked against its hashes):

| Script | What it does |
|---|---|
| `music_organizer.py` | The owner's music organizer, vendored with their two Windows fixes: scan, build a cleaned copy with beets (MusicBrainz, your 14 genres, artwork), swap it in with Navidrome paused and backed up, verify, undo |
| `books_organizer.py` | The owner's books organizer, vendored with their Windows fix: review (titles, series, publishers, genres, covers, misfiled books), apply with Calibre-Web paused, undo |
| `books_run.py` | How the books jobs start `books_organizer.py`: unchanged, but with a time limit on each tool it runs (a minute for `sips` on a cover, an hour for anything else), so a file built to hang one can't hold Tidy Up |
| `books_tidy.py` | Beside it: genre counts from Calibre's database, and **Make a Calibre library** (`build`) for a books folder with no `metadata.db`: each folder holding a book's files moves aside to `<folder> (before Calibre)` on the same drive, `calibredb add --recurse --one-book-per-directory` adds each as one book (its formats together, its `metadata.opf` and cover read) with Calibre-Web paused, and every book is counted in; the copy aside is kept |
| `music_tidy.py` | What Tidy Up adds around the organizer: free the space once verify says every song is there, Navidrome's full scan and missing entries, a smart playlist (`.nsp`) a genre, LRCLIB lyrics, and new music from Downloads |
| `movies_organizer.py`, `tv_organizer.py`, `video_engine.py` | Find the videos that waste space, hand them to Tdarr, and swap each checked result in; file new films as `Title (Year)/Title (Year).mkv` and episodes as `Show/Season 01/Show - S01E01.mkv` (`S01E01-E02` for two in one file, `Show - 112` for anime numbered from its first episode) |
| `audiobook_organizer.py` | Merge a book in many files into one `.m4b` with a chapter a file and one cover (m4b-tool, else ffmpeg); its scan also counts the books that are one file already, so a library with nothing to merge says so |
| `inbox.py` | Watch Downloads and hand each finished download to its engine |
| `scrubber.py` | The nightly sweep of leftovers |
| `tdarr_setup.py` | Tidy Up's two libraries in Tdarr, with the space-saving plugins |

`media_common.py` holds what they share: the Trash on each system, ffprobe, Tdarr's API, and the media servers' refreshes. The vendored scripts are otherwise never edited: `tools/media/tests` fails if either changes. The owner agreed to two fixes on Nov 3, 2026: on Windows, Docker Desktop's names for a mounted folder (`/run/desktop/mnt/host/m/music` for `M:\music`) are read as drive letters, so Navidrome and Calibre-Web are paused while files move; and beets runs on this machine there, since its container can't open Windows folders.

## Settings

`homelab media` writes `data/homelab_media.json` from `.env` before every run (the **Tidy Up** section of Settings): the libraries under `MEDIA_ROOT`, the folders to watch, the languages to keep, Tdarr's codec, where lyrics go, and the address of each media server that is installed. Keys and passwords (`JELLYFIN_API_KEY`, `NAVIDROME_ADMIN_PASSWORD`, `PLEX_TOKEN`, `AUDIOBOOKSHELF_API_KEY`, `TDARR_API_KEY`, `TDARR_USER`, `TDARR_PASSWORD`) reach the engines in their environment, never on a command line or in that file.

Tdarr asks for a sign-in (its accounts are on since 2.4x). Before a job that talks to it (**Set up Tdarr**, a films' or TV's **Shrink** or **Collect**), a Tdarr with no account yet gets one made for Tidy Up through its own sign-up: user `homelab`, a generated password, both kept in `.env` (`TDARR_USER`, `TDARR_PASSWORD`; Settings › Tidy Up), which also sign you in to Tdarr's own page. If you made an account in Tdarr first, put its user name and password there; the engines sign in with them and send its token (`TDARR_API_KEY` still works where Tdarr gives one). Without either, the job says so, plainly.

A new Tdarr's node has no transcode worker, and a node without one takes nothing from its queue: **Set up Tdarr**, a films' or TV's **Shrink**, and **Collect** give each node with none one worker (`/api/v2/alter-worker-limit`; on the graphics card when there is one, else the processor). More can be given on Tdarr's own page; a node that has any is left as it is. Reports go to `reports/media` in the homelab folder, not Downloads, which the inbox watches.

Tdarr's Tidy Up libraries never scan by themselves: Tidy Up hands Tdarr each film or episode that wastes space. **Shrink** reads the videos that came in since the last scan first (Radarr and Sonarr file straight into the library), then hands every one that wastes space. Only what Tdarr's container mounts can be handed: the media drive's `movies` and `tv` folders (`MEDIA_ROOT`), as `/media/movies` and `/media/tv`; a library elsewhere (`TIDY_MOVIES_LIBRARY`, `TIDY_TV_LIBRARY`) is named, with the folder Tdarr can open, and nothing is handed. A video Tdarr still has no record of 20 minutes after it was handed is handed again; after three tries it waits for the next **Shrink**, said on the card, and a new download waiting for Tdarr is filed as it is. **Shrink** stops, saying to run **Set up Tdarr** again, when Tdarr no longer has the library Tidy Up made (its data was cleared, or it was installed again).

## How it keeps out of the way

- **One job moves files at a time:** `data/homelab.media.lock` holds the job's process, and a job that crashed leaves a lock the next run takes over, carrying on where it stopped. A lock whose process ID another program has since been given (one that started after the lock was written, as after a restart) counts as left behind too. While a job this window didn't start holds the lock (the agent's **Collect** every few minutes, a run from Terminal), the screen shows it with its step and bar, read from its log every few seconds, and every button waits, saying for what; once it's done the screen is read again by itself. Both say when the job started, by the day (*Started yesterday at 3:04 AM*). A job that stopped part-way says what it was, with **Carry on**: a step that asks first (a swap, putting the old library back, freeing the space, a books Undo, removing extra copies) asks again; a books apply opens the review instead, since which books you left alone isn't kept with the job; an Undo from **Recently done** is pressed there again.
- **Stop** stops a job at its next safe point, which can take a while: until then the panel says *stopping*. A job that ends without finishing says *didn't finish* and why, with **Try again** (a step that asks first is tried again from its own button). Transcodes aren't under the lock: Tdarr's own queue and its nodes' worker limits pace them; only putting their results in place is.
- **While a job runs,** the nightly update leaves every app that reads a library (Navidrome, beets, Calibre-Web, Komga, Kavita, Audiobookshelf, Jellyfin, the *arr apps, Tdarr) for the next night, `homelab update` refuses them alone, and Duplicati's scheduled backups are paused, then resumed.
- **Lyrics:** each song without time-synced lyrics is looked up on LRCLIB by its exact record (title, artist, album, and length), then, when that has nothing time-synced, by a search on title and artist that takes the same title (without *Remastered*, *Live*, or *feat.*), the same artist, and a length within 3 seconds, time-synced first; the exact record's plain lyrics are kept only when no record has synced ones. Plain lyrics never replace plain ones; instrumentals are skipped. A song's lyrics are read as Navidrome reads them (its `LyricsPriority`: a `.lrc` beside the song, then a `.txt`, then the song's own tags), and written where it will read them: into the song (`TIDY_LYRICS=embed`), or into the `.lrc` beside it when that is set (`lrc`) or when a `.lrc` or `.txt` is already there and would hide the song's own. Lyrics with a translation after a caret on most lines (*¡NUEVAYoL!^NEW YORK!*) keep both languages: each translation goes on a line of its own under its original, with the same time, so a player shows the two together, whether they come from LRCLIB or are already in the song. A song LRCLIB has nothing for is kept in `~/.homelab-media/lyrics-not-found.json` and asked about again after 30 days (or on the next run when the lookup has changed since), and Overview's **Add lyrics** counts only songs not yet asked. **Check lyrics** counts the songs with two languages on one line too, and once every song has lyrics, Overview offers **Tidy lyrics** for them (a check from before it counted them is offered again). The card ends with what came of every song.
- **Power:** building the music library, merging audiobooks, lyrics, and handing videos to Tdarr wait for power and stop at a safe point when a laptop goes on battery; the battery saver pauses Tdarr too, but lets it finish the video it is on first (it takes no new one meanwhile), so no half-made copy is left in its cache; plugged in, it gets its workers back.
- **The agent's minute:** the inbox when a watched folder changed (`TIDY_INBOX_WHEN=arrive`, the default), once after 03:00 (`daily`), or never by itself (`manual`, only on Tidy up now), Tdarr's finished work every few minutes, and the scrubber daily at 03:00. `TIDY_UP=false` turns all three off; `TIDY_INBOX` and `TIDY_SCRUB` each one.
- **Tdarr rests while it has nothing to do:** once Set up Tdarr has made Tidy Up's libraries in it, with no film or episode with it, nothing converting, and no job (nor a start) for 15 minutes, the agent stops Tdarr to free its memory, noting it in `data/stopped.json` as Tidy Up's (`"why": "tidy"`), so the watchdog and the battery saver leave it stopped. Shrink, Collect, Set up Tdarr, filing a film or episode, and a sweep of the watched folders with a video in it start it first (*Starting Tdarr, which was resting*); on battery it stays resting, as the battery saver would have it. A Tdarr someone starts meanwhile stays up until a job next uses it; one a person stopped is never started here. `TIDY_TDARR_REST=false` keeps it running.
- **The \*arr apps rest too** (the owner, Nov 9): Sonarr, Radarr, Lidarr, Prowlarr, Bazarr and FlareSolverr, and qBittorrent with Gluetun, are stopped while nothing is pending, noted in `data/stopped.json` as Tidy Up's (`"why": "tidy"`). Pending is anything in a Sonarr, Radarr or Lidarr queue, a torrent not finished in qBittorrent, or a Seerr request approved and not yet available, and 15 minutes after any of these or after they started; what can't be read counts as pending. A torrent seeding keeps qBittorrent and Gluetun up (qBittorrent is read with `QBITTORRENT_PASSWORD`; without it, it and Gluetun stay up). They wake every 12 hours and ask for an RSS sync and a search of what's missing, at once when Seerr has a request, and before **Hand the libraries to Tidy Up**; on battery, qBittorrent and Gluetun wait for power. One someone starts meanwhile keeps them up until the next 12-hour wake; one a person stopped is never started here. Seerr, Jellyfin and Navidrome stay up. `TIDY_ARR_REST=false` keeps them running.

## Before a run

`homelab media preflight` checks Python (on a Mac, Homebrew's, or Apple's only with its developer tools, so no install prompt appears), the media drive, the container runtime, a graphics card for Tdarr, power, ffmpeg and ffprobe, ffsubsync, m4b-tool, and Calibre's tools, each with its fix. Calibre is found on PATH, as the Mac app, in Program Files, in `/opt/calibre`, in a home folder (where Calibre's installer puts it on SteamOS, whose `/` is read-only), or as the Flatpak from Discover, whose tools it runs through `flatpak run`; `CALIBRE_BIN_DIR` points at another place. ffmpeg from Homebrew or Nix on Linux is found too. Tidy Up's screen shows the same under **This machine**. beets, when no beets image is in Docker, runs from a venv in `~/.music-cleanup/venv` that the first music scan makes; when the Python it was made with is upgraded away (a Homebrew upgrade, say), every run removes the venv whose Python no longer starts, says so, and the engine makes it again, leaving beets' library beside it. The music scripts run straight from Terminal (`music_tidy.py`) do the same before they start.

Without ffmpeg, **Install** (`homelab media install ffmpeg --yes`) gets it with no Terminal and no password. With Homebrew (a Mac, or Linux) or winget (Windows) already there, it runs `brew install ffmpeg` or `winget install Gyan.FFmpeg` itself, each line shown on the card, so ffmpeg is updated with everything else. Without either, it fetches ffmpeg for Tidy Up alone: one pinned build per system (Martin Riedl's for macOS and Linux, Gyan Doshi's for Windows, both GPL), downloaded over HTTPS and checked against its SHA-256 in `internal/media/ffmpeg-pins.json` before anything is unpacked, then run once (`ffmpeg -version`) before it is kept in `~/.homelab-media/bin`. A download that doesn't match is thrown away and never run, and what was there stays. An ffmpeg installed another way (Homebrew, winget, apt) comes first. The pins are written by `go run ./internal/tools/ffmpegpins`, which follows each publisher's latest link to its versioned file and downloads it once; run it to move to a newer ffmpeg.

## Shrink & Strip

New downloads are compared with what the library has. A duplicate replaces the library's copy only when it is leaner for the same picture or sound: AV1 over HEVC over H.264 at the same resolution and length, high-quality AAC or Opus over a huge FLAC; otherwise it goes to the Trash. A download that can't be weighed against the library's copy (that copy can't be read, or the two differ in length, as another cut does) is left where it is, and nothing is replaced; a second version kept on purpose (*Heat (1995) - Director's Cut.mkv*) is never taken for a duplicate. A bloated film or episode waits in a hidden folder in its library until Tdarr's copy is ready, and is filed then. New lossless music is stored as 256k AAC (`TIDY_SHRINK_LOSSLESS=false` keeps it lossless).

A film or episode already in the library twice (the same title and year, or the same episode of a show, and the same length) is found by the films' and TV's scan. `homelab media movies dedupe` (and `tv dedupe`) puts the extra copies in the Trash, after asking: the copy with the best picture stays (the tallest, then the newer codec, then the smaller file), each extra is checked again first (one changed since the scan stays, and so do its copies if the kept one no longer reads), and a version kept on purpose is never one.

TV's scan also says which episodes are missing: a gap in a season's numbering (*S02E01, E02, E05*: E03 and E04), read from the files alone, specials (season 0) left out. One missing after a season's last episode is past what the files can tell.

With Jellyfin set up here (its address and `JELLYFIN_API_KEY`), the films' and TV's scan also asks it which films or shows in that library it shows with no poster. **Fetch posters** (`homelab media movies posters`, `tv posters`) asks Jellyfin to look for those alone again; images are only added, never replaced.

## The download apps

`homelab media arr --apply` (or **Hand over** on the screen) turns Sonarr's, Radarr's, and Lidarr's renaming off, and their Completed Download Handling, which is what moves finished downloads into the libraries. They keep finding and downloading; the downloads stay in their folder, where the inbox files them. LazyLibrarian is told how by hand: turn its post-processing off.
