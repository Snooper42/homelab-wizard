    Changelog¶

    What changed in each version of Homelab Wizard, newest first. Each version's notes are also its release notes on GitHub, and what the app shows before an update.

    Notes are grouped as New, Improved, and Fixed, one line each, in plain words. The pull requests behind a version hold the details. Versions before 0.9.0-beta were tagged afterwards, at the commit where each one ended.

    Unreleased¶

    New¶

    • Lock After in the Homelab app: back within 5 minutes (or 1 or 15, or Right Away, under Settings › This iPhone), the app opens as you left it, Finances' amounts shown or hidden included, without Face ID. Locking the screen still locks it, and a change still asks for Face ID first.
    • Finances reminders for private Finances: the Homelab can't read a household member's private Finances, so the Homelab app on her phone reminds her itself: a bill due tomorrow, a paycheck that leaves checking under her cushion, a yearly renewal, balances not updated for weeks, worked out on her phone with the computer's own code each time she opens them, and planned two weeks ahead.
    • Private Finances offline in the Homelab app: with no signal, or away from home, a household member's iPhone or iPad still opens her private Finances with Face ID, her passphrase or her recovery code, from the locked copy it keeps (only ever locked). A change she makes waits on the device, said in one line, and goes to the Homelab by itself once it answers; if another of her devices changed them meanwhile, the app says so plainly and shows the newest.
    • Private Finances open in the Homelab app: on a household member's iPhone or iPad, Make Them Private… under her Finances (her 16-word recovery code shown once, two of its words typed back, then a passphrase), or on her phone's page; once they're private, Finances shows them locked, and Open with Face ID, her passphrase or her recovery code opens them, on her phone only: they're read forward there with the computer's own Finances code, and saved locked again. After she opens them once with her passphrase or code, Face ID opens them on that device (its key kept in the Keychain behind Face ID, never synced). Lock, leaving Finances, or 5 minutes untouched closes them; what the household shares shows either way. Turn Privacy Off… keeps them plain on the Homelab again.
    • Private Finances offline, on a member's phone page: with no connection (away from home), her phone still opens her private Finances with Face ID or her recovery code, from the locked copy it keeps (only ever locked). A change she makes waits on the phone, said in one line, and goes to the homelab by itself when it answers; if another of her devices changed them meanwhile, the page says so plainly and shows the newest.
    • A household member's phone page shows what the household shares: a read-only Household card under her own Finances (the bills coming up, the subscriptions, this month's budgets), as the Homelab app has, and still there while her private Finances are locked. Turn privacy off… (once she confirms) keeps her Finances plain on the homelab again, receipts included.
    • Private Finances on a household member's phone: on her phone's page, Finances offers Make them private…. Her recovery code is shown once (16 words; she types two back), then Face ID (or a passphrase, where the phone's passkeys can't do it) locks them. From then on the homelab keeps them without being able to open them, the person who runs it included: her phone opens them, reads them forward with the computer's own Finances code, and saves them locked again. Lock (or leaving Finances, or 5 minutes untouched) closes them; Face ID, her passphrase or her recovery code opens them again. The page says its one limit: it comes from the homelab computer, so the Homelab app on her iPhone (next) is the stronger place for them.
    • Finances offline in the Homelab app: with no signal, or away from home, Face ID opens the last Finances your iPhone or iPad read, marked As of when, to look at. Changes wait until the Homelab answers, and then the newest Finances are read by themselves. They're kept locked with a key only that device holds, and go if the phone is unpaired or Finances are taken from it.
    • Add cards from the iPhone and iPad: in the Homelab app, Finances › Collection has Add a Card (and a card's page Edit): its name, set and number, its condition or grade, how many, what one cost and when, what one is worth now and Keep This Price, and a photo of your own, taken with Take a Photo or picked from Photos, shrunk and sent without its location; Remove Card takes it out. Each save asks for Face ID, on a phone allowed to change Finances.
    • The Collection in the Homelab app: on the iPhone and iPad, Finances has Collection: what your cards are worth and the gain on what you paid, each card with its picture (read from the computer), and a card's page with its price over time. Update Prices (on a phone allowed to change Finances, after Face ID) has the computer read the prices from TCGdex. Cards are added on the computer.
    • Update prices for your cards: in Finances › Net worth › Collection, Update prices reads each card's TCGplayer market price from TCGdex, a free card database, and, for a card with no picture yet, its picture, kept on the computer. Only a card's name is sent to find it (then the id TCGdex gives it), never what you paid, how many or anything else of Finances, and only when you press it. A card is matched by its number and set; it says which it couldn't find or couldn't tell apart. A card kept at your own price is never looked up. Each card's page draws its price over time.
    • Your own photo of a card: in a card's form in Finances › Net worth › Collection, Use my own photo takes a picture from your files (or the phone's camera on the phone's page), made small on that device, and the computer keeps it with the card: it shows in the list and large on the card's own page. The pictures are in data/finance-cards/, in the nightly backup; one no card uses any more goes after a day.
    • Your card collection in Finances: Finances › Net worth › Collection lists your cards (made for Pokémon cards, as the mockup drew it): what the collection is worth now, the gain on what you paid and a chart over time, then each card with its set and number, condition, what you paid, what it's worth and its gain. Add a card keeps them in their own Pokémon cards account, so they count in your net worth; a card opens its own page, large. Prices are the ones you type for now: Update prices, with each card's picture, comes next. The phone's page shows it too.
    • Send to my phone and Open on the Computer, in the Homelab app: a page the computer sends your iPhone or iPad shows at the bottom as From the Computer: Radarr with Open. On an app's More About page, Open on the Computer puts the computer's window on that app's page, from a phone allowed to change things (no Face ID, since it changes no data); the computer's window comes forward on it.
    • Send to my phone: right-click an app on Status, pick Send to my phone › and then one of your paired phones, and the page your phone's browser opens shows From the computer: Radarr at the bottom with Open. ⌘K sends the page you're on (Send Finances › Recurring to Sam's iPhone). A wall screen is never offered, Finances goes only to a phone allowed to see them, and a household member's phone only gets Status and their own apps. The Homelab app on the iPhone gets it next.
    • Transactions in columns: on a wide screen, Finances › Spending › Transactions lines each row up in columns (payee, category, account, amount) under a header you click to sort by: the date (newest or oldest first), payee, category or account (A to Z), or amount (most out first, click again for most in). Each device keeps its sort. A narrow window and the phone keep the list as before.
    • The Dashboard looks back: Finances' Dashboard has Checking, last 90 days: what checking held at the end of each day, from the balance you typed in through its transactions, as the account's own page draws it. Pick 30 days or a year, or another account; click a day to open it on the calendar, or the title for the account's page. On the phone's page too.
    • Arrange the Dashboard: on Finances' Dashboard, Arrange the Dashboard lets you move each card (drag its handle, or ▲ ▼), give it a third, half or the whole row, and hide the ones you don't use (they wait below to come back). Reset to the usual puts it back. Each computer keeps its own arrangement; the phone's page stacks its cards as before.
    • A loan's page on the iPhone and iPad: in the Homelab app, a loan's page says what it costs each month, the months left and when it's paid off, and lists the payments linked to it. Link a Payment links one, the same payee's next ones are offered with Link and Not This One, and one that paid more than the plan's payment asks whether to count all of it. Each asks Face ID first.
    • The Watchlist on the iPhone and iPad: the Homelab app's Investments page lists the tickers you follow, with their price and how much they've moved since you followed them; Follow a Ticker looks one up and follows it (after Face ID), and its menu unfollows it. The bar of each holding's share is gone there too.
    • A Watchlist in Investments: follow a ticker you don't own (Follow a ticker under your holdings), and it's listed with its price, a line of it, and how much it's moved since you followed it. Ghostfolio prices it each time it reads your investments. It never counts in your worth. Unfollow takes it off.
    • An account's page on the iPhone and iPad: in the Homelab app, tapping an account on Finances' Accounts page opens its page: what it holds (or owes) today, a chart of it over the last 30 or 90 days or a year, and its latest transactions, with Update Balance at the top. It typed the balance in before.
    • A loan's page, with the payments that pay it: a loan's page in Finances › Net worth says what it costs each month (and the whole payment, when only your share is in the plan), how many months are left and when it's paid off. Under it, Payments linked to this loan: link one with Link a payment… (a transfer to the person you share a mortgage with, say), and the same payee's next ones are offered to link by themselves. One that paid more than the plan's payment asks whether to count all of it.
    • Each account has its own page: in Finances › Net worth, clicking an account opens its page instead of the form to update it: what it holds (or owes) today with a chart over the last 30 or 90 days or a year, what came in and went out this month, and its latest transactions, with All of them in Transactions. Update balance and Edit are buttons there. The same on the phone's page.
    • Settings on the phone's page: the page your phone opens in a browser now has Settings too, as the Homelab app does: the settings a phone may change (never a password or token, nor what the phone connects through), saved from a phone allowed to make changes, and what the computer runs. A phone that only looks reads them; a wall screen doesn't show them.
    • Snooze from Every alert: an alert whose problem is still there now has Snooze… in Every alert, on the computer, the phone's page and the Homelab app, as the Watchdog card has: no alerts about it for an hour or a day, while the watchdog goes on checking. Phones snooze only where they're allowed changes.
    • What was done and Every Alert on the iPad and the Mac: in the iPad's and the Mac app's Homelab, an app's More About now opens beside the list, with Recently Done and Everything That Was Done, and Every Alert under Recent Alerts opens in the app's place until you pick an app again.
    • Links into the app on the iPad and the Mac: a homelab:// link, and with it a button on an alert (Restart, Show Logs, Start), now opens the iPad's and the Mac app's Homelab where it points, as it does on the iPhone: the screen it names in the sidebar, or the app's page beside the list with the action waiting for your tap. Siri's and Shortcuts' Open an App's Page, Restart and the rest do the same there. A link still never changes anything by itself.
    • The Dashboard by month: By month on the bar at the top of Finances' Dashboard steps through calendar months instead of paychecks. A month before this one shows what happened, from your transactions; this month shows what happened so far and what's still to come; a month ahead shows the plan: its paychecks and payments, what comes in and goes out, and checking at its end. Each device remembers which you picked.
    • Look back at a paycheck: on Finances' Dashboard, ‹ in the bar at the top now steps back before today, to the paychecks of the last half year. For each, What happened says what came in and went out, from your transactions (money moved between your own accounts counts as neither), and lists them, and Where it went shows that paycheck's spending by category.
    • Alerts from the Homelab app: Settings › Alerts From on the iPhone picks who notifies that phone: the ntfy app, as before, or the Homelab app, which shows new alerts as its own notifications while it's open. Each phone picks for itself. A locked phone still hears only from the ntfy app until the paid Apple program, so turn the ntfy app's notifications off only if you keep the Homelab app open.
    • Every alert on the iPhone: in the Homelab app, Every Alert under Recent Alerts lists the alerts your computer kept over the last 90 days, each with when its problem cleared or which phone snoozed it, until when.
    • Every alert, kept: the alerts the homelab sends are now kept on your computer for 90 days, not just the 12 hours the ntfy app holds them. Every alert… on Status's Recent alerts card lists them, on the computer and the phone's page, each with when its problem cleared (Cleared at 3:12 AM) or which phone snoozed it, until when. It keeps them whether or not phone alerts are set up. Finances' reminders and the weekly summary are kept only as a plain line, never with an amount.
    • Rules in Finances: fix a transaction once and tick Always do this for “…”: its new name and category go to its other transactions (the category only where they had none), and every import from then on brings that payee in the same way, before anything is guessed. Rules beside Import a file lists them, and Remove takes one away. They're kept in your plan, on your computer.
    • Back and Forward between screens: like a browser, the app now remembers where you've been. ⌘[ and ⌘] (Alt+← and Alt+→ on Windows and Linux), your mouse's back and forward buttons, or a two-finger swipe on a trackpad (right for Back, left for Forward) go back to the screen before, an app's page with its app, and forward again. A swipe over something that scrolls sideways (a wide table) scrolls it instead.
    • Status folds the same on the iPhone too: the Homelab app now shows the groups folded as the computer keeps them, and a group you fold or open on the phone (one allowed changes) folds or opens on the computer and the other phones.
    • Status folds the same everywhere: the groups you open or fold on Status (Media, Photos, Security…) are now kept by the computer, so the app and the phone's page show them the same way. Show every app stays each device's own, and Fold them again puts every device back to the usual (groups with a problem open, the rest folded). A phone that can only look keeps its own folds.
    • Home only: each app that can be on the internet (Paperless, Vaultwarden, Immich and the like) has Reach it away from home on its page, on the computer and in the iPhone app, once your domain has the Cloudflare tunnel. Off keeps it home only: it opens on your home Wi-Fi, and nobody outside can reach it, because its address comes off the tunnel and Cloudflare's DNS (only what the homelab put there; anything you added yourself stays). homelab away off paperless does the same. Apps you install from now on start home only; the ones you already have stay as they were.
    • What was done, on the iPhone: an app's More About page shows what was done to it lately, and Everything That Was Done searches the last 90 days, as on the computer.
    • Recently done, on each app's page: the last few things done to it (Restarted Vikunja, 2 hours ago, by the watchdog), and Everything that was done…, the last 90 days for every app, searchable by app, phone or word.
    • A record of what was done: every start, stop, restart, update, roll back and removal, the watchdog's restarts, settings saved and Finances changed are kept for 90 days, with who did it (this computer, which phone, the watchdog or the nightly update). homelab history lists them, --app immich or --search narrows them; the app and the iPhone show them next. It never keeps a setting's value or an amount of money.
    • The download apps rest when nothing is on the way: Sonarr, Radarr, Lidarr, Prowlarr, Bazarr, FlareSolverr, qBittorrent and its VPN tunnel used to sit in memory all day. Now, with nothing downloading, queued or requested for 15 minutes, Tidy Up stops them, and starts them every 12 hours to look for new releases, at once when you request something in Seerr, and whenever Tidy Up needs them. A torrent still seeding keeps qBittorrent up. One you start yourself stays up until the next 12-hour wake. TIDY_ARR_REST=false in Settings keeps them running.
    • Buttons on alerts: an app down now reaches your phone with Restart and Show Logs (or Open Status when it's more than one), and an app stopped for restarting over and over with Show Logs and Start. A tap in the ntfy app opens the iPhone app on that app's page, where the action waits for your tap and Face ID: the button never changes anything by itself.
    • Links into the iPhone app: homelab://app/radarr opens Radarr's page, homelab://finances/budgets opens Budgets, and homelab://app/radarr?do=restart opens Radarr's page with Restart waiting for your tap. A link never changes anything by itself. They're for the buttons on alerts, coming next.
    • Away mode on the iPhone: when neither home nor the tunnel answers, the app no longer just says it can't reach the computer: it lists your apps that are on the internet (Immich, say), as it last saw them, each with whether it answers and a tap to open it.
    • The iPhone app away from home: once the computer is set up for it and the phone is paired again at home, the Homelab app shows Status, your apps, and the Finances it may see on cellular too, through your Cloudflare tunnel, sealed so that even Cloudflare can't read it. At home it goes straight to the computer as before. Away, it only looks: changes wait until you're home.
    • Reach Finances away from home: a Setup step that puts Cloudflare Access in front of the phone companion's own address (homelab-away.<your domain>), letting in only your household's phones, and publishes it through your tunnel, all with the Cloudflare token you already have (given two more permissions). Phones paired at home afterwards get what they need to reach it; the iPhone app's part comes next.
    • The iPhone app away from home, the computer's part: the phone companion can now answer the iPhone app through the Cloudflare tunnel, for looking only (Status, your apps, Finances it may see), behind Cloudflare Access's service token, Cloudflare's signed pass, the phone's own pairing, and a key the phone and the computer agree at home, so even Cloudflare can't read what passes. Nothing changes until Setup's Reach Finances away from home turns it on (next), and the iPhone app learns to use it after that.
    • Tdarr rests while it has nothing to do: Tdarr, which shrinks films and episodes, used to sit in memory all day. Now, with no video to shrink and nothing done for 15 minutes, Tidy Up stops it, and starts it again by itself when it has one (Shrink, a big download to file). The watchdog and the battery saver leave it resting; one you start yourself stays up until Tidy Up next uses it. TIDY_TDARR_REST=false in Settings keeps it running.
    • Packed downloads say so: a .rar or .7z dropped in a watched folder (or a download that's only .rar parts) no longer sits there unexplained: Needs a look lists it, says it's packed, and that what's inside is filed once you open it. Leave it stops it asking.
    • Look a film or show up: in the Inbox's Needs a look, It's a film… and It's an episode… now have Look it up: Radarr, Sonarr, or Jellyfin find what you typed, with its year, so you pick The Big Lebowski (1998) instead of typing the year. You can still type it yourself.
    • Name these songs: songs that don't say which artist or album they are no longer get filed into a folder with no name: they wait in the Inbox's Needs a look (with AcoustID set up, only the ones beets can't recognise by their sound). Name these songs… asks the album, and the artist if you know it, looks it up on MusicBrainz (Rumours · Fleetwood Mac · 1977 · 11 songs · Vinyl, US), and once you pick the one they are, writes its names onto them and files them in Music. Songs that don't fit the album you picked (a different count, or lengths far from its songs) stay, saying why.
    • Where should your libraries live?: the first time, Tidy Up shows your computer's disk and each drive plugged in, with its space and the libraries already on it, and where each library would go. Pick one (or another folder, such as a NAS), and Tidy Up keeps it and restarts the apps that read your libraries, after listing them. Nothing is moved or copied. Change where your libraries live… on the Inbox opens it again.
    • A loan read from its papers: give a loan the day its first payment was due and each payment (and, if you like, how many there are and the finance charge) and Finances works out what it owes each day, taking each payment off the day after it's due, with no balance to type. Its card on Net worth shows a ring of the payments made (3 of 36), what's paid down of what you borrowed, the next payment split into what pays it down and its interest, who pays it each month, when it's half and wholly paid off, and every payment in a table. With the finance charge, the first payment carries the days before it, so the 36 come to your papers to the cent.
    • Your last six months on Cash flow: under the Dashboard's typical month, a bar of money in beside money out for each of the last six months your transactions cover; pick a month for its transactions.
    • Spent this month on the Dashboard: beside your latest transactions, a ring of what your transactions spent since the 1st by category, largest first; pick a category for this month's transactions of it.
    • Latest transactions on the Dashboard: Finances' Dashboard lists your five newest transactions, each opening its own form, with See all for Transactions.
    • What a bill was paid lately: open a payment on Finances and its form lists its last few from your transactions, each with how much more or less it was than planned, and their average when they differ (Paid lately, $141.17 on average). An account's form likewise lists its balances lately, each with how it moved.
    • A running balance: pick one account on Finances › Transactions and each day says what it held after (or, for a card, what it owed), worked from the balance you last typed in through the transactions in and out of it.
    • Add on a calendar day: the day you pick on Finances › Calendar has Add a payment (a day ahead) or Add a transaction (today or before), its form starting on that day. T takes the calendar back to today.
    • A month in all, on the calendar: beside the day you pick, Finances › Calendar sums up the month shown: what its paychecks bring, what its bills take, the lowest checking gets and on which day (said when it's under your cushion), and what your transactions recorded so far. On a narrow window it comes first.
    • What happened, on the calendar: Finances › Calendar now shows the days before today too, each with its payees' logos and what it came to from your transactions; pick a day for the list, each opening its transaction, and go back a month for what it came to, as far back as your transactions go.
    • Needs a look: a download Tidy Up couldn't place (a video whose name says nothing, say) waits on the Inbox with why, instead of sitting in Downloads unexplained. Tell it It's a film… or It's an episode… and type the name: Tidy Up renames it and files it. Leave it stops it asking, and Show in Finder opens the folder. Something you put back with Undo shows here too.
    • What I did today: Tidy Up's Inbox lists each download it filed today in plain words, like Heat (1995) · Movies › Heat (1995) · too big, so Tdarr is shrinking it first or 12 songs, tags cleaned · lyrics added where found, newest first. Undo on one puts its files back where they came from, and Tidy Up leaves them there.
    • Drop anything here: Tidy Up opens on its new Inbox: the folders it watches for music, films, shows, books and audiobooks (Downloads to start; Add… a folder on your external drive too), when it tidies them (As things arrive, Once a day, 3 AM, or Only when I press it), and one Tidy up now button. A watched folder on a drive that's unplugged says so.
    • Add an account from the iPhone: Finances › Accounts › + adds one (its name, its kind, and what's in it or owed today), and a plan with none yet opens on Add an Account, so a household member can start their own Finances from their phone.
    • The iPhone app on a household member's phone: on a phone the computer gave to someone in the household (homelab phone person), Status shows only their apps, Finances is theirs ("My Finances"), with a Household card for the bills, subscriptions and budgets you share with them, to look at, and Settings is just their phone. Backups and Setup stay yours.
    • Month in Review on the iPhone: Finances › Month in Review tells each month back, one page each (swipe for the ones before): what you spent and how that compares with the month before, where it went, what came in and what was left, what grew and what shrank the most, and the biggest purchase.
