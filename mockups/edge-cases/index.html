Round 6The keyboard: where the focus is, and where it goes

Round 5 (dates, big monitors) is built and waits for its turn to merge. This pass pressed Tab through every screen on Windows and Mac, in light, dark and a contrast theme, and checked each stop for a visible focus ring. 1,103 of its 1,104 stops show one. It also checked the one menu and the sheets, and found three things.

16In a contrast theme, the current screen and the focus look different

In a Windows contrast theme the sidebar marks the current screen with an outline, the same outline that keyboard focus uses. With focus on Backups, Settings looks focused too; with focus on Settings, nothing changes. Proposal: in a contrast theme, the current screen gets a bar in the highlight colour at its left edge (as Windows' own sidebars do), and focus alone gets the outline, in the theme's text colour.

17Tab stays inside an open sheet

A sheet (Update all apps, Fix it, an app's log) dims the screen behind it, but Tab walks straight out of it: after Cancel, the next stops are the sidebar and the buttons behind the dimming, which can be pressed with Enter while the sheet is still open. Proposal: while a sheet is open, Tab goes round its own controls only (after the last, back to the first; Shift+Tab the other way), and the screen behind can't take focus. Escape and the focus going back where it was on close stay as they are.

18Status's More… menu works from the keyboard

More… opens with Enter, but the arrow keys don't reach its items, and Tabbing past them leaves the menu open over the cards with the focus somewhere else. Proposal: the keys every menu takes: Down (or Enter) opens it on its first item, Up and Down move through the items, Escape closes it and puts the focus back on More…, and the menu closes as soon as the focus leaves it.

Round 5Other languages, and very wide windows

Round 4 is merged (#303, #305). This pass ran every screen with the computer set to German, Japanese, French and British English, at text size 0.8 and 0.9 on Mac, Windows and Linux, and on a 2560 px monitor. Text size 0.8 held up everywhere. Two things came up.

14Dates in English, in your order and on your clock

The app speaks English, but it writes some dates in the computer's language. On a Mac set to German, an app's page says "Missed checks on Dienstag" and "Restore test passed 18. Okt.", with week bars marked S S M D M D F. In Japanese it says "火曜日" and "10月18日". The app is also inconsistent with itself: the savings trend always writes US dates and the crash note always British ones. Proposal: every date in the app goes through one rule. The words are English, the day comes before the month unless your computer puts the month first (the US), and the clock is 24-hour or AM/PM as your computer's is. In the US nothing changes ("Oct 18", "3:28 AM").

15On a big monitor, each screen sits in the middle

Screens stop growing at 1280 px, so they don't turn into long lines of text. But they sit at the left, and on a 2560 px monitor the right 40% of the window is empty. Proposal: the screen keeps its width and sits centered in the space beside the sidebar. Nothing changes in windows narrower than that.

Round 4After 0.15.0: contrast themes, keyboard, and fingers

Rounds 1 to 3 are merged and shipped in 0.15.0. This pass drew every screen in a Windows contrast theme, tabbed through every control on Mac, Windows and Linux (focus showed on all of them, in contrast themes too), and measured what a finger has to hit on the phone. Two things came up.

12Switches show On and Off in a Windows contrast theme

A bug: in a Windows contrast theme, a switch (an app's Nightly updates and Tell me if it stops, and the switches in Settings) draws as an empty outline whether it is on or off. Its white knob sits on the theme's white background. Proposal: in a contrast theme a switch takes the theme's own colours. Off is an outlined track with a dark knob on the left; On is the highlight colour with the knob on the right.

13On the phone, buttons a finger can hit

Apple asks for touch targets of at least 44 points. In the phone companion, Open and Start are 25 px tall, the chevron to an app's page is 30 px, a category's fold is 20 px, the switches are 32×19 (an iPhone's own are 51×31), and "‹ Status" is 20 px tall. Proposal, on the phone only: buttons 36 px tall with a little more side room, the chevron and folds 40 px, switches the iPhone's size, and the back link a taller target. The desktop app does not change.

Round 3One more, after rounds 1 and 2 shipped

Rounds 1 and 2 are built and merged (#293, #295 to #299). A third pass covered the first run, Choose stacks' categories and custom app form, every Settings page, pairing a phone, the crash note, running jobs, empty cards, kiosk mode, and the phone at 320 px. Everything held up except one thing.

11Settings' page list becomes one drop-down when space is short

At text size 1.5 or larger in a small window, Settings' list of pages (This Mac, Storage, Appearance, People… and every app category) wraps into a block about six rows tall above the page. You scroll past all of it to reach a setting, and again after each pick. Proposal: when the screen is that narrow, one Page drop-down replaces the list, grouped as the list is (This Mac, Household, Reaching the apps, Apps). In a normal window, nothing changes.

Round 2More edge cases, found after you approved round 1

Round 1 (below) is approved and being built: PR #293 is open for 1 and 2, and 3 to 5 are ready behind it. A wider pass then opened every Settings page, sheet and menu, drew the app at text size 2 in big windows, and tried the phone companion at 320 px. Six more findings are below. The first three are bugs in the app today.

6Sheets stay inside the window at big text

At text size 1.5 in the smallest window, the sheets (Update apps, Open on a phone, an action that failed) are wider than the window and cut off on both sides: the title and the Update them button are hidden. The cause is that the sheet's size ignores the text size. Proposal: the sheet keeps its look, sized to the window at any text size.

7The update line keeps Update now in reach

At text size 1.5 or larger in a small window, the line at the top that says a new version is ready cuts off Later and Update now. Proposal: when the line runs out of room, its buttons go on a second line under the words. A very long version name wraps rather than hiding them.

8Two columns only when there is room for two

Choose stacks (the list and its plan), Status' paired cards, and an app's page (log and settings side by side) decide on one or two columns by the window's width, ignoring the text size. At text size 2 in a 1440×900 window, Choose stacks' app list is 84 px wide beside a 340 px plan. Proposal: the same columns, chosen by the room the screen has at the text size, as the tiles now are. At text size 1 nothing changes.

9On the phone, an app's address gets its own line

In the phone companion, each app's address squeezes beside its state, so it breaks into a narrow column ("npm. / example. / com") even on a usual iPhone. On a small phone (320 px), a long address shows one letter per line. Proposal: the address goes on its own line under the state, full width. Open and the chevron stay on the name's line.

10A long search wraps

Searching Choose stacks for something long without spaces (a pasted link, say) makes the screen scroll sideways, because “No app matches …” cannot wrap. Proposal: it wraps.

Round 1Approved, being built

1The sidebar turns into a row of icons when space runs short

At text size 1.5 or larger in the smallest window, the sidebar keeps its full width and every screen gets squeezed. At size 2, Status has 218 px left and scrolls sideways. Proposal: once the window has less than about 700 px of room at the chosen text size, the sidebar shows only the icons. The counts sit on the icons, the screen names show on hover and are still read by screen readers, and the version line goes away. A wider window or smaller text brings the full sidebar back. The phone keeps its tab bar.

The rail on the other systems, text size 1.5:

2Nothing spills out at large text

  • A screen's toolbar stays one capsule. Today Backups' “Back up now” drops to a second line inside the box.
  • Summary tiles go to one column when a screen is narrow, and the line under a tile wraps. Today “+$301 since Aug 31” is cut off.
  • Backups' database table drops its “7 kept” column when narrow.
  • The Settings and Choose stacks search boxes stay inside the screen.
  • On Setup, a step's buttons move under its words when the row is narrow, so the step's name keeps one line.

3Long names and addresses wrap instead of pushing the page

A long app name or a long address (for example a deep subdomain on your own domain) makes the app's page scroll sideways, even in a big window. The address is cut to “todo.examp…” and the Remove button grows past the edge. Proposal: the address wraps after its dots, as it already does on Status. The title wraps with the status pill below it, and Remove's label wraps.

4A brand-new homelab says what to do first

With no apps yet, Status says “0 of 0 · All baseline apps are up”, shows an empty table header and a “Fold them again” link with nothing to fold. Proposal: one card, “No apps yet”, with a Choose stacks button, in place of the tiles and the empty list. On Backups, the copies tile says “None yet” instead of “0 of 0”, and the restore test tile stops saying “Never” over “Proven in the last 30 days”, which is a real contradiction in the app today.

5Windows high contrast keeps the bad day visible

In a Windows contrast theme, an app page's “This week” bars all draw as the same empty outline, so the day with missed checks disappears. Proposal: good days are solid, and a day with missed checks is hatched in the theme's highlight colour. Everything else checked fine in high contrast: the active screen, buttons, meters and dots.
